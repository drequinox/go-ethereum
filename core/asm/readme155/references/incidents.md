# Incident lessons

Real losses show where adversaries actually go. Use these to prioritise attack paths and to justify recommendations. Facts below were checked against the linked sources on 17 October 2026. When citing an incident in a review, cite its source, and re-check figures if the review goes to a risk committee or regulator.

| Incident | What happened | Lesson | Check to apply | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bybit, 21 February 2025, over USD 1.4 billion (401,347 ETH and other assets) | Malicious JavaScript was injected into the Safe{Wallet} web interface through a compromised Safe developer machine. Signers believed they were approving a routine transfer; the transaction used delegatecall to overwrite the wallet proxy's implementation address with an attacker-controlled one. Attributed to the North Korean Lazarus group. | A quorum is defeated when every signer trusts the same interface | Independent transaction decoding on a separate path; block or escalate delegatecall and implementation changes; integrity of front-end code served to signers | [NCC Group analysis](https://www.nccgroup.com/research/in-depth-technical-analysis-of-the-bybit-hack/) |
| WazirX, July 2024, about USD 230–235 million | A 4-of-6 Safe multisig (five keys held by WazirX on hardware wallets, one by the custody provider Liminal). The transaction looked benign in the custody interface but its data switched the wallet to an attacker contract deployed eight days earlier, bypassing both the multisig and the address whitelist. | What approvers see must be bound to what they sign; implementation changes bypass every other control | Display integrity; simulation before signing; dangerous-call guard and time delay on implementation changes | [Halborn analysis](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-wazirx-hack-july-2024) |
| Radiant Capital, 16 October 2024, about USD 50 million | A developer received a Telegram message appearing to come from a trusted former contractor, with a zipped PDF; it carried a backdoor that spread to three developers' devices. The Safe{Wallet} interface showed legitimate transactions while malicious ones were signed. Mandiant attributes it with high confidence to North Korea-linked UNC4736. | Signer devices are part of the custody boundary; standard checks and simulations showed nothing wrong | Dedicated signing devices; verification on a hardware wallet screen; device and software diversity across approvers | [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/radiant-capital-50-million-heist-blamed-on-north-korean-hackers/) |
| DMM Bitcoin, late May 2024, 4,502.9 BTC (about USD 308 million) | The FBI attributes it to North Korea's TraderTraitor group. A fake LinkedIn recruiter gave an employee of DMM's wallet software vendor (Ginco) a "pre-employment test" containing malicious code; the attackers later impersonated the employee and manipulated a legitimate transaction request. | Vendor staff and vendor systems are part of the attack surface | Vendor trust model and access scope; independent verification of requests that pass through a vendor | [BleepingComputer, citing the FBI](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/fbi-links-north-korean-hackers-to-308-million-crypto-heist) |
| Ronin bridge, 23 March 2022, 173,600 ETH and 25.5 million USDC | Attackers obtained five of nine validator signatures: four Sky Mavis validators after compromising Sky Mavis systems, and one Axie DAO validator through an allowlist arrangement that had ended but was never revoked. Discovered six days later, after a user could not withdraw. Sky Mavis confirmed initial access by spear-phishing of an employee; press reports of a fake job offer rest on anonymous sources. | Threshold without independence is not a threshold; stale delegations persist; detection matters | Quorum independence; periodic review and revocation of delegations; alerting on signing outside the expected path | [Ronin official post](https://roninchain.com/blog/posts/community-alert-ronin-validators-6513cc78a5edc1001b03c366) |
| Harmony Horizon bridge, 23 June 2022, about USD 100 million | The bridge's multisig needed two of five keys; the attacker controlled two and drained the bridge directly. | Low thresholds fail | Threshold such that threshold-minus-one compromises cannot sign | [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/154029/harmonys-100-million-hacker-took-control-of-its-multi-signature-wallet-analysts-say) |
| Ledger Connect Kit, 14 December 2023 | A former employee was phished and their npm publishing access, never removed at offboarding, was used to publish malicious versions containing a wallet drainer. Live for about five hours; Ledger estimates active draining under two hours. | Offboarding must cover external tools; third-party front-end code can turn every user into a victim within hours | Offboarding checks for external publishing rights; pinned and verified dependencies; release signing; clear signing over blind signing | [Ledger incident report](https://ledger.com/blog/security-incident-report) |
| Coinbase, disclosed 15 May 2025, estimated USD 180–400 million in costs | Overseas support staff were paid to take customer data from internal systems: names, addresses, phone numbers, email, last four digits of Social Security numbers, masked bank details, ID images, balances and transaction histories, for under 1% of monthly transacting users. The data was used to make scams more convincing, tricking customers into sending funds. Coinbase refused a USD 20 million ransom. Passwords and private keys were not taken. | Support staff and support tools are part of the attack surface; client data enables theft without touching keys | Least-data support tooling; bulk-access monitoring; two-person rule for sensitive support actions; client communication on how the firm will contact them | [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/05/15/coinbase_extorted_for_20m_support/) |
| BitForge disclosure, August 2023 | Fireblocks showed key extraction from MPC wallets: GG18 and GG20 implementations that did not check the Paillier modulus (CVE-2023-33241), and Lindell17 implementations that kept signing after aborts (CVE-2023-33242). Several major providers were affected; some patched, others reportedly had not at disclosure. | MPC security depends on the implementation, not the protocol label | Name the protocol and implementation version; require evidence that the fixes are in, and an independent audit | [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/cryptocurrency/new-bitforge-cryptocurrency-wallet-flaws-lets-hackers-steal-crypto/amp/) |
| Wormhole bridge (Solana), 2 February 2022, 120,000 wETH (about USD 326 million) | The Solana side of the bridge used a deprecated function, `load_instruction_at`, that did not verify the Instructions sysvar account. The attacker passed a fake sysvar account, so forged guardian signatures were treated as verified, and minted 120,000 wETH. | Account validation is the core of Solana program security; one unverified account defeats signature verification | Program audits cover signer, owner and sysvar account validation; limit exposure to any one bridge | [Halborn analysis](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-wormhole-hack-february-2022) |
| Slope wallet (Solana), early August 2022, nearly 8,000 wallets, about USD 4 million or more | Per Otter's investigation (not confirmed by Slope), the Slope mobile app sent users' seed phrases over TLS to a centralised Sentry server operated for Slope, where they were stored unencrypted. | Telemetry and logging are an extraction path for keys | Secrets are excluded from logs and telemetry by design and tested; third-party SDKs inside key-handling code are reviewed | [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/161425/slope-wallet-provider-saved-user-seed-phrases-in-plain-text-solana-security-researchers-find) |
| `@solana/web3.js` compromise, 3 December 2024 (CVE-2024-54134) | A publish-access account was compromised and versions 1.95.6 and 1.95.7 were published with code to steal private keys, live between 15:20 and 20:25 UTC. Apps handling raw keys, such as bots and backend signers, were at risk; the advice was to upgrade to 1.95.8 and rotate exposed authority keys. | A core chain library is part of the custody boundary for any service that holds keys | Pinned, verified dependencies; no raw keys in application processes; key rotation runbooks | [GitLab advisory](https://advisories.gitlab.com/npm/@solana/web3.js/CVE-2024-54134/) |
| TSSHOCK disclosure, Black Hat USA 2023 | Verichains showed key extraction by a single malicious party after one or two signatures, through ambiguous encoding in proof transcripts and weakened discrete-log proofs. Verichains states that most implementations of GG18, GG20 and **CGGMP21** were vulnerable; some libraries were fixed, at least one was not. | Even newer protocols are only as safe as their implementation | As BitForge; also ask whether keys were regenerated after patching, as Verichains advises | [Verichains](https://www.verichains.io/tsshock/) |

## Patterns across these incidents

1. **The interface is the target.** Attackers compromise what signers see, not the keys themselves (Bybit, WazirX, Radiant).
2. **People, vendors and dependencies are the entry point.** Fake recruiters, fake contractors, vendor staff, bribed support staff and compromised packages appear repeatedly (DMM, Radiant, Ronin, Ledger, Coinbase, `@solana/web3.js`).
3. **Common-mode failure beats thresholds.** Many approvers on the same software, devices or vendor count as one (Bybit, Radiant, Ronin).
4. **Implementation and upgrade paths hand over everything.** One signature on an implementation change is total loss (Bybit, WazirX).
5. **Detection is slow.** Ronin went unnoticed for six days.
6. **MPC is implementation-sensitive.** Protocol names are not assurance (BitForge, TSSHOCK).
7. **Chain-specific validation fails silently.** Every chain has its own way to break verification or leak keys (Wormhole account validation, Slope telemetry). See [chains.md](chains.md).
